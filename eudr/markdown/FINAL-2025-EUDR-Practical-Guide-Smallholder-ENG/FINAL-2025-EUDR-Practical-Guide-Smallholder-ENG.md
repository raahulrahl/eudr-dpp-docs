# Introduction

## Purpose of the Practical Guide

This Practical Guide is here to help you understand and get ready for the European Union Regulation on Deforestation-free Products (EUDR).

The EUDR is designed to cut down the global impact of deforestation linked to key commodities, many of which are produced by smallholders like you. Meeting these requirements is essential to ensure deforestation-free production. It can also open new doors for your deforestation-free products and help you boost productivity through more sustainable practices.

By working through this guide, you will learn exactly what the EUDR requires, how to prepare, and where to and support. The aim is simple: to help you succeed in a market that values legality, sustainability, and traceability.

## What is the EUDR?

The EUDR is an EU regulation aimed at helping to minimize deforestation worldwide. It ensures that products entering the EU, i.e. coffee, cocoa, palm oil, rubber, soy, cattle and timber are produced in a manner that does not harm forests. The goal is to support sustainable farming, protect the environment, and make supply chains more transparent, allowing people to trace the origin of products and their production process.

This regulation officially came into force on 29 June 2023 and is set to become fully applicable from 30 December 2025 (or 30 June 2026 for Small and Medium Enterprises/ SMEs).

## EUDR Key Definition

![](_page_7_Picture_30.jpeg)

### 01. Deforestation-free

Products must not come from land that was cleared of forests after December 31, 2020.

![](_page_7_Picture_33.jpeg)

### 02. Operator

Any person or company who sells products to the EU market or exports them.

![](_page_7_Picture_36.jpeg)

### 03. Legality

The product must be produced in line with the laws of the country where it was grown.

![](_page_7_Picture_39.jpeg)

### 04. Sustainability

Farming methods must care for the environment and protect natural resources for the future.

![](_page_7_Picture_42.jpeg)

### 05. Polygon

Digital boundary of a farm or plot of land, usually represented as a geospatial file.

## Why EUDR Matters for Smallholders Farmers

The EUDR brings new opportunities for smallholder farmers in Indonesia who grow palm oil, rubber, coffee, soy, timber, cattle, and cocoa.

Buyers now look for proof that these products come from legal and deforestation-free farms. You can build stronger trust with buyers, open doors to new markets, increase your reputation, increase your unique selling proposition, enhance the resilience for your community, and benefit from new market options.

![](_page_7_Picture_48.jpeg)

## Benefits of compliance

EUDR compliance brings some opportunities. When you follow the regulations, you can access the EU markets that favor sustainable products and may receive support programs and training to Improve farming practices on their farms. EUDR compliance also offers further benefits, namely fairer prices, substantiated concerns addressed, stronger and independent position in value chain. EUDR is interesting for farmers who are not yet certified. Ultimately, aligning with EUDR can help you build sustainable livelihoods while protecting Indonesia's forests.

If you cannot meet the EUDR rules, you may lose access to the EU markets, which could reduce your income.

### Legality

You may be asked to show that your land use complies with Indonesian laws. This can include having appropriate land ownership or land use documentation, and ensuring farming practices follow national regulations related to:

### Traceability

To meet EUDR-related expectation, your buyers may asked you to provide geolocation data of plots of production, i.e. geolocation point for plots <4 ha or polygon for plots >4ha. This geolocation data may also be collected from STDB (Surat Tanda Daftar Budidaya) mapping process.

This information helps buyers demonstrate that the product comes from land that is deforestation-free and legally managed.

Based on the Regulation of the Director General of Social Forestry and Environmental Partnerships Number: P.15/PSKL/SET/PSL.0/11/2016 concerning Online Services for Social Forestry, the process of obtaining a Social Forestry Decree (SK Perhutanan Sosial) is as follows:

## Farm Registration on e-STDB

![](_page_19_Picture_20.jpeg)

STDB (Cultivation Registration Certificate) is a cultivation statement issued to farmers by the Regent and/or Mayor

e-STDB is the electronic system of the Cultivation Registration Certificate (STDB).

![](_page_19_Figure_23.jpeg)

![](_page_19_Picture_24.jpeg)

The electronic system integrates a national database of smallholder (independent) farmers across Indonesia.

![](_page_19_Picture_26.jpeg)

This system enables the government and stakeholders to easily monitor farmers who are officially registered in plantation cultivation activities.

![](_page_19_Picture_28.jpeg)

The issuance of the STDB is not categorized as a legal document. but relevant as part of documentation under EUDR for smallholder ownership of plantations.

![](_page_19_Picture_30.jpeg)

Farmers criteria: Farmers who own and/or manage less than 25 (twenty-five) hectares of land.

## Benefit of STDB to Farmers

- Proof of active farmer participation in legitimate cultivation activities
- Improved access to commodity supply chains
- Enhanced credibility and bargaining position within the agricultural value chain
- Qualification standard to receive financial and technical support from both government and private sectors

## 01 SOCIALIZATION

![](_page_20_Picture_21.jpeg)

- • The e-STDB dissemination is carried out by the government, companies, and/or development partners to agricultural extension workers, plantation facilitators, village officials, farmer group leaders, farmer group association leaders, and representatives from plantations that have plasma or supported farms.
- • Dissemination can be conducted through workshops, meetings, focus group discussions (FGDs), leaflets, or banners.

## 02 DATA COLLECTION

![](_page_20_Picture_24.jpeg)

- • The district/city data collection team gathers information on farmers and their farms through direct census or by collecting data from farmer groups/Forest Management Units (KPH) or the Directorate of Social Forestry and Environmental Partnership (BPSKL) prior to the census.
- • The team uploads all collected farmer and farm data into the e-STDB system

## 03 MAPPING

![](_page_20_Picture_27.jpeg)

- • The district/city mapping team clearly maps farmers' plantations, identifying land boundaries using GPS or other mapping tools.
- • The mapping team ensures that the plantation plots align with spatial planning regulations and forest boundaries.
- • The team uploads all collected plot maps into the e-STDB system.
- • This geolocation data from STDB mapping is useful for EUDR traceability requirement.

## 04 VERIFICATION

![](_page_20_Picture_30.jpeg)

- • The verification team formed by the district/city government checks the farmer, plantation, and map data that has been uploaded to the e-STDB system.
- • Verification is also conducted through overlay analysis between the plantation location map and the forest area/concession (HGU) maps.
- • For commodities located within forest areas, the verification team also checks the documentation of land access rights for farmers in forest zones.
- • If necessary, the verification team may conduct field visits and meet farmers directly to validate the data and maps (on-site method).
- • Verification can also be conducted independently on the e-STDB platform, which has integrated forest area maps and HGU maps (on-desk method).

## 05 ISSUANCE

![](_page_20_Picture_33.jpeg)

- • Farmer data and farm maps that have been verified as clean and clear (CnC) are then processed for the issuance of the STDB through electronic signing by the regent/mayor.
- • The issuance may be delegated to the head of the agency responsible for plantation affairs at the district/city level.

## Fair Farming Practices

Fair farming practices protect workers and meet labor laws to support ethical and legal production

### Prohibit Hazardous Child Labor

Do not allow children under 18 to handle sharp tools, heavy loads, or chemicals.

Children may help with light tasks under supervision.

![](_page_22_Picture_30.jpeg)

### Ensure Fair Wages and Work Conditions

Assign only light, non-hazardous tasks to pregnant women (e.g., no heavy lifting or pesticide use).

Ensure workers are treated fairly, without discrimination, and can request adjustments if needed.

![](_page_22_Picture_34.jpeg)

### Community Grievance Mechanisms

Create a trusted contact person or grievance box in the village or cooperative.

## Work with Buyers

### a. Ask buyers what records are needed for EUDR

Some usually required data or documents are:

- Farm location (GPS points for farm <4 ha or polygon coordinates for farm >4 ha)
- Land ownership or legal documents (e.g. SHM, SK Perhutanan Sosial, STDB)
- Crop type and planting year
- Harvest dates and quantity
- Sales and transaction records (buyer, quantity, price, date)

Some data and documents are usually helpful to prepare for buyers:

- Environmental license
- Photos of farm activities or fields
- Certification documents
- Input use (fertilizer, pesticides)

### b. Request training or tools

You can request training or tools from buyers, such as smartphone apps or GPS devices for plot mapping. You should retain control over their own data.

### c. Negotiate support in exchange for higher-quality supply

You can use your improved practices, accurate records, or higher-quality products as a basis to ask buyers for additional support, such as training, tools, or technical advice.

### Join a Cooperative

**a. Ask village leader or extension officer which cooperatives are active.** They can advise which cooperatives are trusted, have experience supporting smallholders, and are involved in relevant supply chains.

---

**b. Fill in registration form.** To join a cooperative, you usually need to complete a registration form and provide basic documents such as your ID (KTP) and land information or farm registration records. Some cooperatives may also ask for proof of participation in government programs like STDB.

---

**c. Attend regular meetings and trainings.** Attending regular meetings and training sessions helps you stay updated for about good farming practices, market requirements, and EUDR compliance, learn from peers, and improve your farm management.

## Farm Certification Programs

While certification programs (e.g. Rainforest Alliance) under the EUDR do not automatically guarantee compliance, they can be valuable tools for smallholders, by:

- Helping farmers collect and organize important information, such as farm geolocation, land legality documents, and production practices, which are required for EUDR compliance.
- Providing templates, training, and verification systems that support traceability and legal verification.

Certification alone does not replace the buyer's due diligence, so farmers must ensure that all data is complete and accurate.

## Glossary

### Aggregator

A group, cooperative, or business that collects products (like rubber, cocoa, or coffee) from many small farmers and then sends them to factories or exporters.

### Batch ID / Batch Code

A special number given to a specific amount of product, so it can always be traced back to the farm it came from.

### Deforestation

The conversion of forest to agricultural or other non-forest land use, whether human-induced or natural.

### Deforestation-Free

A requirement under the EUDR meaning that the commodity was not produced on land deforested after 31 December 2020.

### DDS (Due Diligence Statement)

A formal declaration sent to the EU system (TRACES) that confirms your products are legal, deforestation-free, and follow the rules.

### Due Diligence

A legal requirement to check and collect information about your farm and suppliers, make sure there is no illegal deforestation, and take action if there is risk before selling to the EU.

### EUDR (European Union Deforestation Regulation)

An EU rule (Regulation 2023/1115) that prohibits products linked to deforestation from being sold or exported to Europe.

### Forest

Land larger than 0.5 hectares with trees taller than 5 meters and canopy cover over 10%, or land that can grow such trees, excluding farms or urban areas.

### Forest Degradation

Structural changes to forest cover, taking the form of the conversion of:

1. (a) primary forests or naturally regenerating forests into plantation forests or into other wooded land; or
2. (b) primary forests into planted forests;

### Forest-Related Rules

National laws about using forests, clearing forests, or cutting timber, as required by EUDR.

### Geolocation

The exact GPS coordinates (latitude and longitude) of your farm plots.

### Indigenous Peoples' Rights

The rights of indigenous communities whose land or resources may be affected, including FPIC (Free, Prior, and Informed Consent).

### Legality Documentation

Papers that prove your land ownership or rights, environmental permits (like SPPL), and tax compliance.

**Operator**Any natural or legal person who, in the course of a commercial activity, places relevant products on the EU market or exports them.

**Polygon**A shape made by connecting GPS points that shows the full boundary of a farm plot. Required for farms bigger than 4 hectares.

**Risk Assessment**The process of checking how likely it is that a supplier or product does not follow EUDR rules.

**Risk Mitigation**Actions to reduce risk, like extra inspections, farm visits, or training suppliers.

**Supplier**Anyone who provides products or raw materials to an operator, including cooperatives and aggregators.

**Traceability**The ability to track a product from the farm all the way to the buyer.
