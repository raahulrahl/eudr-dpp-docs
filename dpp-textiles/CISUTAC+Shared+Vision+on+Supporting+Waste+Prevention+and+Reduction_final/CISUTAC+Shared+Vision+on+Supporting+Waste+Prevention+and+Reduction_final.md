# CISUTAC Vision for a Circular and Sustainable EU Textile Sector: Supporting waste prevention and reduction

The CISUTAC project aims to increase circularity and sustainability in textiles and clothing in Europe. Its main goal is to remove current bottlenecks in textile management to increase circular use of textiles in the EU, and to minimise the total environmental impact of the sector. Within CISUTAC, partners are developing a shared vision for a more circular and sustainable EU textile sector to advise on strategic directions to identify relevant innovation gaps and possible solutions emerging from CISUTAC.

At the top of the waste hierarchy, waste prevention measures are pivotal to achieving the textile sector's transition towards circularity. The Waste Framework Directive defines it as "measures taken before a substance, material or product has become waste, that reduce: (a) the quantity of waste, including through the reuse of products or the extension of the life span of products; (b) the adverse impacts of the generated waste on the environment and human health; or (c) the content of harmful substances in materials and products."<sup>1</sup> Furthermore, the role of prevention and reuse of textile waste represents a significant potential for reducing greenhouse emissions.<sup>2</sup> CISUTAC provides policy recommendations supporting waste prevention and reduction and advances their realisation by filling technological and knowledge gaps through several pilots and activities.

### **Tackling overproduction**

The main environmental and climate impact of textile products is generated in the production phase, accounting for about 80% of their life-cycle greenhouse gas emissions, and also requires large amounts of energy and water and uses a variety of chemicals. <sup>3</sup> At the same time, the EEA estimates that 4-9% of all products put on the market in Europe end up being destroyed without ever being used for their intended purpose.<sup>4</sup> It is therefore important to reduce the overproduction of textile products. # **Extending and intensifying use**

Other waste prevention measures that could be applied at the production phase look into the way products are designed. Rethinking the production of textiles through ecodesign measures can help prevent the generation of waste if criteria focus on quality and circularity, effectively preserving the value of materials for as long as possible through reuse, repair and recycling. Durable products should be designed as easy to repair, maintain, and recycle, and be accompanied by care/repair instructions guiding the consum-

er on how to extend the life of their products. New products should incorporate recycled contents to the extent possible in line with technological developments and market growth, and being mindful of potential trade-offs, for example between durability and use of recycled contents.

Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) fees should be modulated based on environmental considerations, i.e. eco-modulation, to promote more sustainable production and the uptake of recycled contents. To achieve the biggest impacts, eco-modulation should be based on ESPR eco-design criteria and uniformly implemented, to incentivise companies to produce more circular and durable products.

# **Guiding a behavioural change**

An important role in preventing textile waste can be played by targeting citizens' behaviour. On the one hand, information requirements (delivered by the Digital Products Passport) and the use of standardised labels could enable consumers to make informed choices.

Additionally, local authorities should be supported to implement measures such as communication campaigns that can raise citizens' knowledge of textiles circularity and awareness on the importance of sustainable and responsible consumption and the impacts of textile waste.
