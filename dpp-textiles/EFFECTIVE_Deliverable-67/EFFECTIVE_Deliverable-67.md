## <span id="page-4-0"></span>**1. Introduction**

The present report constitutes deliverable 6.7 "Report on the results obtained out of surveys and interviews on the biobased products" within the framework of the EFFECTIVE project. The following activities refer to WP6 "Resource efficiency, environmental sustainability and social acceptance" and specifically to task 6.3 "Social acceptance of the biobased products.

The objective of this report is to address social acceptance issues toward the future market uptake of the biobased products. To achieve this, the following activities have been carried out as part of T6.3:

- Revision of the background on social acceptance of bio-based products from past projects in the EU.
- Design of the EFFECTIVE Survey, one per type of product or material: (1) Garments, jacket for outdoor activities and cycling shorts from VAUDE and sportswear and swimsuit from H&M; (2) carpets for the automotive and construction sectors from BALSAN; (3) material for food packaging and (4) materials for secondary packaging
- Translation of the EFFECTIVE Survey into French, German, Swedish, Italian, Croatian, Slovene, and Spanish.
- Launch of the EFFECTIVE Survey within the EUSurvey platform, followed by a second launch within the Pollfish Survey.
- Collection of 1059 responses form 14 European countries.
- In parallel to the EFFECTIVE survey, 4 workshops on social acceptance of biobased products were organised and carried out. The dates and venues were as follows: o 1st EFFECTIVE Workshop. CIRCE Headquarters, Zaragoza, Spain. 19th of June 2019. o 2nd EFFECTIVE Workshop. National Environment Congress - CONAMA 2020, Madrid, Spain. 2nd of June 2021. o 3rd EFFECTIVE Workshop. Online. 15th of June 2022. o 4th EFFECTIVE Workshop. Online. 23rd of June 2022.

Despite the limitations to carry out participatory activities imposed by the COVID-19 pandemic, the present document reports the results of the above-mentioned activities regarding the social acceptance of biobased products. Recommendations and insights to support their future market uptake are also commented at the end of the document.

### <span id="page-5-1"></span>**2.1 EFFECTIVE Surveys**

### <span id="page-5-2"></span>*2.1.1 Background and design*

First, a preliminary revision of the market status at EU level of the sectors involved within EFFECTIVE (construction, automotive, textile and packaging) was carried out to set a geographical scope to disseminate the survey. Diverse sources were checked to better understand the market size and importance of each sector for each country in comparison with the rest of EU country members. The market relativeness, also taking in mind the products to evaluate, wasthen valorised in a scale from 1 (low) to 3 (high). This information was complemented with the direct input from partners. Each of them was asked to indicate the countries in which they could have representativeness (physical store or distribution channel of final products, etc.), considering their specific role as value chain actor of the project. The final weight of each region was assessed combining the preliminary market revision per sector/product, the country representativeness of each partner (also considering the language of the survey) and the actual country of origin of all the partners. This resulted in the identification of the most relevant regions where the survey is conducted: France, Germany and Italy resulted as the top 3, followed by Netherlands, Spain, Sweden, Croatia, Slovenia, Poland, and Belgium.

Second, for the design of the questions, insights achieved in past projects involving biobased products were revised. The results of Open-BIO project "Opening bio-based markets via standards, labelling and procurement" that had the participation of NOVAMONT, were complemented with the conclusions of the projects innProBio "Forum for bio-based innovation in public procurement", BioWays "Bio-based economy: network, innovate, communicate", Agrimax "Agri and food waste valorisation co-ops based on flexible multifeedstocks biorefinery processing technologies for new high added value applications". Other circular economy projects in which CIRCE took part as partner such as CIRCPACK "Towards circular economy in the plastic packaging value chain" and AGROinLOG "Demonstration of innovative integrated biomass logistics centres for the Agro-industry sector in Europe" are also consulted. This literature revision resulted in the identification of topics to include, having in mind already identified market barriers and social expectations, and the specific approach that EFFECTIVE activities should adopt in the case of each target group.

Finally, after several iterations, the definitive version of the EFFECTIVE Survey (see Annex 1) was defined among T6.3 partners. 4 surveys were designed, one per EFFECTIVE material and/or product: (1) Garments: Jacket for outdoor activities and cycling shorts from VAUDE 0F0F <sup>1</sup> and port wear and swimsuit from H&M1F1F 2 ; (2) Carpets for the automotive and construction sectors from BALSAN2F2F 3 ; (3) Material for food packaging3F3F <sup>4</sup> and (4) Materials for secondary packaging4F4F 5 .The definitive questionnaire was divided into the following sections:

- A general section to compile the key aspects that consumers evaluate in each product, as well as the level of understanding of the term bio-based, and what is considered to be a bio-based material or product.
- Analysis of consumers' perceptions of factors that would influence their preference (or not) for organic products. It asks about accessibility to information and availability in local shops, as well as product aspects such as quality, environmental performance, and price.
- Evaluation of EFFECTIVE's bio-based products compared to conventional ones

## <span id="page-34-0"></span>**4. In-person and Online Workshops**

As shown in [Table 1,](#page-10-0) a total of 4 workshops were held during the EFFECTIVE project, divided equally into two formats: in-person and online. In any case, both typologies had a common goal and similar dynamics. The main objective of these sessions was to collect, in an interactive way, the responses of the representatives of each stakeholder group, mainly from companies in the sector and public administration, but also from consumers as the events were open to everyone interested, on different aspects related to the sustainable production of the bio-based products presented in the project. In this way, the aim was to identify barriers and opportunities for the replicability and uptake of the proposed solutions.

In addition to the project's presentation and project's partners showcase of partial results, the events were also focused on the discussion about audience's opinions on the subject. This is done with a dynamic activity based on a voting session. A series of multiple-choice questions were asked to attendees about barriers, opportunities, information requirements and so on, and, after the voting, results were presented immediately to foster the discussion and to hear the reasons behind the choice of each person.

In the case of the post-survey events (2 nd , 3 rd, and 4th workshops), these questions were linked with the ones from the surveys. So, when results were presented, the debate wasset in both the reasons behind attendees' responses, and the concordance (or not) with the results from the survey. The results of the surveys were displayed with the twofold aim of, in one hand, deliver partial and interesting results for the people in the session and, on the other, to foster the debate regarding the differences and similarities between the two information sources. Finally, the moderator drew a short conclusion of the results at the end of each question. A summary of these results is reported as follows.

**Benefits from EFFECTIVE:** most of participants feel that EFFECTIVE's innovations help to reduce the impact associated with products made from polyamides and polyester. They highlight that the best steps towards this would be prevention, recycling, and the use of sustainable raw materials. In the case of garments, aspects such as energy efficiency with respect to conventional ones, the lack of political will, the lack of knowledge of what happens with textile waste, or the higher valuation of price over quality are highlighted.

**Awareness level**: in general, people taking part in the session show a higher level of awareness that those interviewed through the survey. Stakeholders of the packaging sector present a higher level of awareness as there are no completely unaware participants, unlike with textiles-related ones where this was the case for almost 15% of attendees.

**Role of public administrations:** in general, participants agreed that the public administrations should take the lead in raising consumer awareness and engagement. The main instruments to be used should be regulation, followed by education and information campaigns.

**Information barriers:** overall, attendees from the packaging sector agree that keep track on the innovations from the bio-based industry is a high effort-demanding task. Behind the reasons for this, they identified the lack of related advertisement, the low interest or time available for this in comparison with daily activities, and the absence of an easy-to-reach data base or similar. Whereas stakeholders attending the textile workshop comment that the three key aspect for their sector are the "overuse of "bio" terms that make people stop caring", followed by "people do not understand/care how this product is made" and "lack of information in the product to differentiate it as labels, product brochure, etc."

**Information needs towards product uptake**: in terms of the type of information of most interest to stakeholders, it could be seen that CO2 savings would be among the most important in both cases. Only in the case of packaging it would be tied with "Life cycle benefits compared with a not bio-based and similar product" and both surpassed by "Biodegradability/Recyclability properties", while in the case of textiles, it would share the first position with "Life cycle benefits compared with a not bio-based and similar product", surpassing "The percentage of bio-based material" and "Social impact of the production" with a small difference.

**Products exploitable strengths**: In order to promote these products and improve their sales, workshop attendees felt that in the case of packaging all options ("green public procurement would accelerate the market uptake of it", "discourage unwanted market behaviours with fiscal measures such as taxes, charges, fees or fines", "establish a framework regulation that support the use/procurement of it" and "the use of a certified label or the possibility to have a certification") are almost equally good, with the exception of "give financial support in form of grants, subsidies or investments" and "promote public-private partnerships that prioritize the use of this products" which received hardly any votes. For garments, "certify that at least 50% of the product is bio-based" was considered the best method for this promotion, while for carpets "promote public-private partnerships that prioritize the use of this products and "establish a framework regulation that support the use/procurement of it" would be the best.

**Product weaknesses to address** when asked what would be most disapproved of in this type of product, the packaging workshop attendees clearly saw "increase the complexity of the recycling" as the main aspect to avoid, reaching 50% of the votes (this issue is commented below). In the case of textiles, the aspect to avoid is "higher prices for a product with similar functionalities".

**Waste management and recyclability:** One of the main barriers identified during the sessions is the high cost and increase of complexity for the improvement of the waste management system. The introduction of new materials is perceived as a challenge given that many of them might not be compatible with the services and the technologies in place within the recyclability value chain. Although this is a technical issue (e.g. the upgrade of recycling facilities), other impacts downstream, such as where this waste should be collected, or if these new materials are compatible with current waste flows, are also commented for sellers and buyers

**Willingness to buy/use bio-based products:** Finally, in both cases, all participants showed that they would prefer to buy these bio-based and more sustainable versions, rather than the more conventional ones.

## <span id="page-36-0"></span>**5. Conclusion**

The present report delivers the results obtained out of surveys and interviews on the biobased product. The approach that stakeholders show towards the various products demonstrated by EFFECTIVE is reflected in the information gathered though the survey and workshops. These insights are useful to select the most suitable strategies for the social acceptance. In this sense, the main learnings are summarised as follows:

**Awareness:** From the first questions, it can be observed a lack of knowledge on the part of the participants, as acknowledged by themselves. The majority believes that all bio-based products have the property of biodegradability. In general, all agree that the main reason the public/private sector is unaware of the existence of these products is the lack of publicity. This is quite clear in the case of the packaging products, while for the other two products, this position is rivalled by other reasons. In the case of carpets this is "Keep track of "bio" innovations demand a great effort", while for garments it is due to both "Overuse of "bio" terms make people stop caring" and "People do not understand/care how this product is made". This view is shared by younger people but is becoming less and less important until it is overtaken by the age of 45, and even then, it is still considered one of the main reasons. The same is true for countries, where the majority considers "Lack of advertisement in the streets, media, website" to be the most important. In view of this lack of knowledge, it is essential to solve this solution, which, given the results, would imply at least investing in advertising through different media.

**Information:** In terms of what to communicate to stakeholders to increase social acceptance, there is clearly a broad consensus that the information that institutions/organizations are most concerned about right now are those properties related to the end of life of the product, i.e., its recyclability or biodegradability, as well as CO2 emission savings compared to conventional alternatives. Except for carpets, where "Biodegradability properties" is outperform by "The CO2 savings in respect of a conventional alternative" considerably, all other products present balanced values for both cases. In this case there is no influence of age or country of origin, except for the Croatians who value "The percentage of bio-based material" above all else, while "The CO2 savings in respect of a conventional alternative" is one of the least interested. As these properties have some reputable certifications, their use could be useful for reporting and accreditation purposes.

**Promotion:** Since reporting does not at once lead to the purchase of the product, a question was asked about the best method to convince institutions/organisations to purchase these products. For carpets and secondary packaging there is no one method that stands out above the others, as the responses are fairly balanced, while for garmentsthe most important are discounts or vouchers for future purchases and for food packaging the promotion of public-private partnerships that prioritise the use of these products. In terms of age, younger people are more likely to opt for financial support such as scholarships, grants, or investments, while as they get older, interests become more focused on other aspects such as "Promote Public-Private partnerships that prioritize the use of this products" and "Green Public Procurement would accelerate the market uptake of it". As far as countries are concerned and despite their individualities, all agree that "Promote Public-Private partnerships that prioritize the use of this products" is among the best methods to promote the sale of such products. Most significant is the fact that contrary to the general opinion where "Give financial support in form of grants, subsidies or investments" is among the best ways to achieve such goal, for BE and FR, they would have hardly any interest. In summary, the type of product, as well as the age and, in some cases, the country of origin of the target buyer, will have to be considered, but from a general perspective it can be concluded that the promotion of public-private partnerships that prioritise the use of these products will be one of the best ways to promote the sale of these products.

**Benefits:** Without any influence from age or country, all agree again that the most important benefits for institutions from these products are a reduced environmental impact, as well as a reduction in fossil fuel consumption and gas emissions. This corresponds to the interest shown in receiving information about CO2 emission savings compared to conventional versions. So, it can be concluded that these products emitting as little as possible would be the most desired property for the stakeholders.

**Barriers:** For all products, the fact that the products were more expensive than others with similar functionalities cause the most disapproval. There seems to be no influence of age or origin, other than that "Increase the complexity of the recycling process" in SI is equally important as "Volatile and/or higher prices for a product with similar functionalities", while in HR it is even more important.

In summary, to design and develop the most proper strategiesfor the social acceptance of the new bio-based products, an advertising campaign through different media will be needed to address the lack of awareness observed in the results of the surveys. In turn, this campaign should focus on informing about the end-of-life properties of the product, as well as the savings in CO2 emissions compared to the conventional version. This is due to the great interest shown by the survey participants in these properties. One way of doing this could be by promoting the acquisition of certificates or labels.

However, the promotion of these products through a campaign cannot be limited to information. To be effective, it must be accompanied by some other action. When choosing this action, distinct aspects must be considered, such as the type of product to be sold or the age/origin of the target buyer. Finally, for this campaign to be effective, it will be significant that the price of the bio-based product does not exceed that of the conventional product.
